# Disclosure audits on certain features

When auditing website forms that collect personal information, look for two distinct types of checkboxes:

## 1. Privacy Policy acknowledgment

Does the form include an unchecked, required checkbox asking the user to acknowledge the Privacy Policy?

Example: "I agree to the Privacy Policy."

Is the Privacy Policy hyperlinked to the actual privacy policy page of the website?

Encourage website owners to implement this feature to document that users have been presented with and have affirmatively acknowledged the Privacy Policy before submitting their personal information.

Encourage website owners to maintain a record of the acknowledgment, including the timestamp and version of the Privacy Policy presented.

Encourage website owners to ensure these checkboxes are:

- Unchecked by default.
- Required to be checked prior to ever allowing a user to submit their personal data.
- Separate from any other consent checkboxes.

Certain privacy laws require organizations to obtain meaningful consent for the collection, use and disclosure of personal information, subject to applicable exceptions.

Encourage website owners to implement an unchecked-by-default, required checkbox stating "I agree to the Privacy Policy" on forms that collect personal information.

When supported by clear and appropriate privacy disclosures, this mechanism can help website owners obtain and document affirmative consent to the collection and processing of personal information.

Encourage website owners to maintain records of the user's affirmative action, timestamp, consent language and applicable Privacy Policy version. Consent for additional processing activities, such as email or SMS marketing, should be obtained separately where appropriate.

The absence of a Privacy Policy agreement checkbox does not automatically indicate a legal violation, and the presence of one does not automatically establish legal compliance. Encourage website owners to consult with their privacy attorney regarding applicable consent requirements.

## 2. Consent to specific processing activities

If the website collects personal information for additional purposes, such as email or SMS marketing, identify whether the form provides separate consent checkboxes for those activities.

Examples:

- "I agree to receive marketing emails."
- "I agree to receive marketing text messages."

Encourage website owners to ensure these checkboxes are:

- Unchecked by default.
- Optional and not required to submit an unrelated inquiry.
- Separate from the Privacy Policy acknowledgment checkbox.
- Specific about the activity to which the user is consenting.
- Supported by appropriate consent recordkeeping.

Recommend that website owners consult with their privacy attorney to determine the appropriate disclosures and consent mechanisms for their specific business practices and applicable laws.

## AI feature disclosures

If the website uses an AI widget or feature, such as an AI-powered chat widget, identify whether visitors are clearly informed that they are interacting with artificial intelligence.

Encourage website owners to provide clear disclosures when visitors interact with AI-powered features, particularly when visitors may otherwise believe they are interacting with a human. Privacy and artificial intelligence laws, including the EU AI Act, may impose transparency and disclosure obligations relating to certain uses of artificial intelligence.

Identify whether AI disclosures exist and flag potential applicable transparency obligations. Recommend that the website owner consult with their attorney to determine what specific disclosures are required.
